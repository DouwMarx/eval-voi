The top ten of Table~\ref{tab:ranking} is a recognizable economic type: a
routine, commercially available instrument costing tens to a few thousand
dollars --- a credit-bureau inquiry, an employment verification, a laboratory
assay, a sensor reading --- attached to a decision whose stakes are two to
three orders of magnitude larger, with an elicited prior in the middle band
where neither acting nor not acting dominates by default. Four clusters
recur: consumer credit decisions (an employment-verification check before a
personal loan; a soft credit inquiry before a store card), acute-care
medicine (genomic risk stratification before a transplant decision; serial
troponin testing in chest-pain triage), agricultural quality control (soil
pathogen assay, seed germination testing, grape maturity sampling), and
industrial safety or asset decisions (laboratory gas analysis before an
emergency response, load-profile analysis before replacing a transformer).
The stored reasoning strings show the mechanism directly: the elicitor prices
the instrument at its market rate, prices the decision's dominant loss at a
claim, outage, or crop scale, and lands on an efficiency far above one.

The ranking's head deserves suspicion in proportion to its interval widths.
The top-ranked scenario has $P_+ = \voiTopPpos$ but a q05 of zero, and its
stored reasoning also illustrates a failure mode the validators cannot catch:
in one repeat the benefit $B$ is priced as the lender's interest income from
an approved loan rather than as the regret difference the model defines ---
a quiet re-anchoring of the utility convention. By contrast, the genomic
risk-stratification scenario is the sturdiest entry in the list: it is the
only scenario whose efficiency stays above 1 at its 5th percentile
(Table~\ref{tab:ranking}), because both the stakes (transplant-or-not with
survival differences priced in the hundreds of thousands) and the assay cost
(a few thousand dollars) are comparatively well-pinned quantities.

Two structural observations from the bottom of the ranking balance the
picture. First, \voiZeroEvsiCount{} of \voiNRanked{} scenarios have median
EVSI of zero, and the ones that stay dead across protocol variants share a
signature: the prior already dictates the action (a track-safety check before

frontier-AI capability evaluation is the instructive near-miss: its median
EVSI is zero under every protocol variant except one, because whether the
deployment-gating response is already optimal at the prior sits on a knife

intuitively ``important'' measurements are worthless per-decision because
nobody's action hinges on them. Second, scenarios whose single decision touches many people
at once (a city air-quality alert) earn large $B$ legitimately --- one
decision, population-scale consequences --- which the per-measurement framing
rewards without any market-size unit error.
