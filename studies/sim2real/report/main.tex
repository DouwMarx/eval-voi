\begin{abstract}
Robot safety evaluations range in fidelity from text prompts to monitored deployment. We give a protocol for asking when a higher-fidelity rung pays for one release decision: price each rung, a pass/fail report at a preset mark, by its expected value of sample information (EVSI) minus its cost. We work it through on a home vision-language-action (VLA) manipulator ladder (\twNHome{} rungs) and a pedestrian automatic emergency braking (AEB) ladder (\twNAeb{} rungs), with every input elicited from Claude models. The elicited inputs imply that a pass/fail home rung can change the launch only if the prior probability of an unsafe mode lies between \twHomeBandLoMin{} and \twHomeBandHiMax{} (\twHomeBandHiLSeven{} for lab trials) at the elicited stakes; the elicited prior moved from \twHomePSingleLo--\twHomePSingleHi{} to \twHomeP{} with the prompt, and at \twHomeP{} no pass/fail reading from nine of the ten rungs could change the launch. Where the decision is open (AEB), the track test has the highest net value under every elicitor set with Sonnet or Opus, and fleet telemetry cannot pay over it. Inside the gate a step pays when $pB\,\Delta s+(1-p)K\,\Delta t$ exceeds its extra cost, a line a paired sim-to-real study can test.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{Safety evaluation, Value of information, Sim-to-real, Elicitation}

\section{Introduction}

Robot foundation models are evaluated with instruments of very different fidelity, from text prompts to a language planner~\citep{obi2025safeplan} through adversarial scenes~\citep{majumdar2025predictive}, closed-loop simulation~\citep{fan2026safevlabench} and world models~\citep{geminirobotics2025veo} to lab trials~\citep{zhang2026redvla} and deployment monitoring. The sim-to-real gap is usually studied as a validity question: does a simulated score predict the real one~\citep{kadian2020sim2real,li2024simpler,gervet2023navigating,dauner2024navsim,luo2025sim2val,stocco2023mindgap}? Automated-driving assessment combines simulation, track and road tests~\citep{reckenzaun2024natm} with leading and lagging measures~\citep{fraadeblanar2018measuring}. Other work spends a budget across fidelity tiers to estimate a metric or find failures: multi-fidelity, transfer, prediction-powered and sequential estimators~\citep{peherstorfer2018survey,winkelmann2022transfer,angelopoulos2023ppi,mahboob2026betting,snyder2026beyond,kressgazit2024empirical}, black-box and rare-event falsification~\citep{corso2021survey,okelly2018scalable}, accelerated autonomous-vehicle testing~\citep{glasmacher2023acquire,feng2023dense,yang2022sparse} and screen-then-confirm tiers~\citep{barrett2024benchmarkearly}; autonomous real-robot evaluation cuts the cost of the real rungs~\citep{zhou2025autoeval,atreya2025roboarena}. Whoever decides on release, through a readiness determination or a safety case~\citep{webb2020waymo,ul2020ul4600,buhl2024safetycases}, needs a different answer: can any rung change the decision, and by more than it costs~\citep{raiffa1961applied,howard1966information,koopman2020positive}? Health economics prices studies by their expected value of sample information (EVSI)~\citep{ades2004evsi}; safety cases that combine cheap offline tests with few costly real ones~\citep{sinha2026rethinking,shevlane2023extreme} need the same price per rung.

We contribute a protocol for pricing an evaluation ladder for one release decision (box) and work it through on a home manipulator ladder (levels 0 to 9) and a pedestrian automatic emergency braking (AEB) ladder, the control, whose test-to-field chain is published~\citep{iihs2024paeb,cicchino2022pedestrian}. The model prices only the go/no-go role of an evaluation; sim-to-real validity enters it only through each rung's Youden index $J=s+t-1$, which also mixes hazard coverage and exposure. All inputs are elicited from language models, so each result is what the elicited inputs imply: (i) on the AEB ladder the track test has the highest net value under every elicitor set with Sonnet or Opus, and fleet telemetry cannot pay over it; (ii) whether a pass/fail home rung can change the launch turns on the prior against the decision threshold; both moved across elicitors, and the prior moved with the prompt; (iii) a ladder comparison needs the decision's numbers elicited without the instrument text (Table~\ref{tab:robust}).

\noindent\fbox{\parbox{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}{\small\textbf{Using the protocol.} (1) Gate: would a flag make you delay and a pass make you release? If not, the rung cannot change this decision. (2) Net value: among rungs in the gate buy the one with the highest $\evsi-C$, or none if all are negative. (3) Bounds, for any $s,t$: rule out a rung costing more than $\evpi$, the value of perfect information, and a step costing more than $\evpi$ minus the cheaper rung's $\evsi$.}}

\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

One agent faces one binary decision: respond ($a=1$: delay the release and mitigate) or not. The state $\theta=1$ means responding is correct: the policy has an injurious behaviour mode, or the AEB misses a crossing pedestrian. In regret form $u(\theta,0)=0$, $u(1,1)=B$, $u(0,1)=-K$. The evaluation flags or passes at a preset mark, $x\in\{0,1\}$, with sensitivity $s=P(x{=}1\mid\theta{=}1)$ and specificity $t=P(x{=}0\mid\theta{=}0)$; the prior is $p=P(\theta{=}1)$; $C$ is the cost to build the evaluation from scratch and run it once. With stakes $\Lambda=B+K$ and threshold $\pist=K/\Lambda$, acting on belief $\pi$ is worth $V(\pi)=\Lambda\,[\pi-\pist]^+$, and one run is worth~\citep{raiffa1961applied}
\begin{equation}
\evsi = P_1V(\pi_1)+P_0V(\pi_0)-V(p)\;\le\;\evpi=\min\{pB,\,(1-p)K\},
\label{eq:evsi}
\end{equation}
with $P_1=ps+(1-p)(1-t)=1-P_0$, $\pi_1=ps/P_1$, $\pi_0=p(1-s)/P_0$ and EVPI the expected value of perfect information.
\textbf{Assumptions.} The agent buys at most one rung for this decision (rungs are substitutes) and maximises the net value $\evsi-C$, the one-decision form of the expected net benefit of sampling~\citep{willan2005value,conti2009dimensions}; it buys none if every net value is negative. Left out: trial counts, sequential use, and finding, fixing and re-testing failure modes, which would add value to cheap rungs, and systematic sim-to-real bias~\citep{kennedy2001calibration}, which would lower their $s$, $t$ and value.
\textbf{The gate}~\citep{howard1966information,pauker1980threshold}. For $s+t>1$, $\evsi>0$ only if $\pi_0<\pist<\pi_1$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:gate}); otherwise the agent acts the same whatever the reading: the gate is closed. Equivalently, $p$ lies in the rung's gate band, $\mathrm{odds}(p)\in\big(\mathrm{odds}(\pist)\,(1-t)/s,\ \mathrm{odds}(\pist)\,t/(1-s)\big)$, the testing thresholds of clinical decision analysis~\citep{pauker1980threshold}; outside it the test's net benefit is that of ``treat all'' or ``treat none''~\citep{vickers2006decision}. The gate comes from the preset mark, whose likelihood ratio is bounded by $(1-s)/t$ and $s/(1-t)$; a continuous reading with an unbounded likelihood ratio (e.g.\ Gaussian) has no gate, nor does a mark on it set for this decision.
\textbf{The decision's break-even line.} Inside the gate $\evsi$ is linear in $s$ and $t$: $psB-(1-p)(1-t)K$ for $p<\pist$ and $(1-p)tK-p(1-s)B$ for $p>\pist$. So a step from rung $a$ to a costlier rung $b$, both in the gate, pays if
\begin{equation}
pB\,\Delta s+(1-p)K\,\Delta t>\Delta C .
\label{eq:line}
\end{equation}
\textbf{The best-placed buyer.} At fixed stakes $\evsi$ peaks for the buyer whose threshold is the prior ($\pist=p$, the fence buyer), whose two weights in~\eqref{eq:line} are both $\Lambda p(1-p)$: its value is the fence value $\evsi^\star=\Lambda\,p(1-p)\,J$, stakes times the variance of the state times Youden's index $J=s+t-1$~\citep{youden1950index}. For this buyer a step pays if it raises $J$ by more than $\Delta J^\star=\Delta C/(\Lambda p(1-p))$: the target for the most value any buyer with these stakes could get, not for this decision.

\section{Design and elicitation}
\label{sec:design}

\textbf{Ladders} (App.~\ref{app:catalogue}). Home: a company's head of safety decides whether to delay the launch of a VLA mobile manipulator into homes and add mitigations. Rungs (L$k$ is level $k$): text prompts (L0), image and video question answering (QA), generated adversarial scenes, a closed-loop MuJoCo suite (L4), a world model or digital twin, a hardware-in-the-loop twin, lab trials with contact-force measurement (L7), a field test in real homes (L8) and a monitored pilot deployment (L9). AEB: a carmaker decides whether to delay a pedestrian AEB release; rungs: vision-language model (VLM) QA on camera frames (L1), virtual testing (L4), vehicle-in-the-loop (L6), a track test (L8) and fleet telemetry (L9). Background paragraphs come from a catalogue of \twNCatalog{} papers and were fact-checked (\twFactClaims{} claims, \twFactEdits{} edits).
\textbf{Elicitation} (App.~\ref{app:protocol}). Language models can supply priors for statistical models~\citep{capstick2024autoelicit,selby2025experts}; we use them as structured expert elicitors~\citep{ohagan2006uncertain}. One prompt per scenario, model and repeat asks for the 5th, 50th and 95th percentiles (p5, p50, p95) of each parameter against two worked anchors; Beta ($p,s,t$) and lognormal ($B,K,C$) fits follow. The headline protocol is staged: $p,B,K$ once per decision from a context without instrument text, $s,t,C$ once per rung without decision-level numbers. It pools Claude Sonnet 5 and Opus 5.5 (\twModelSonnet, \twModelOpus), \voiMemberKA{} repeats each (USD \voiElicitCost); Haiku 4.5 and a single-prompt protocol are baselines (App.~\ref{app:iterations}).
\textbf{Point estimate.} We evaluate~\eqref{eq:evsi} at the pooled medians of the p50s (the plug-in point); a bootstrap over elicitations (\voiBootN{} replicates) measures the agreement between them. The predictive means, the principled point for a risk-neutral decision-maker, rest on fitted lognormal tails ($B$, $K$ at \twMeanRatioLo{} to \twMeanRatioHi{} times their medians) and leave the AEB order and the home verdict unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:robust}).

\textbf{On the AEB ladder the track test has the highest net value.}\label{sec:aeb} The AEB release is open ($p=\twAebP$, $\pist=\twAebPiStar$) and every rung is in the gate. The track test (L8) has the highest net value, \twAebNetLEight{} USD, and fleet telemetry (L9) the lowest, \twAebNetLNine{} (Table~\ref{tab:net}). Under the staged prompt each step up to L8 pays (\step{1}{4} in \twStepOneFourPays{} of bootstrap replicates); under the single prompt the simulated rungs lose money, but L8 still leads (Table~\ref{tab:robust}). No $(s,t)$ could rescue fleet telemetry at the median stakes: $\evpi-\evsi(\mathrm{L8})=$ \twAebHeadroom{} $<\Delta C=$ \twStepEightNinedC.
\textbf{Worked example: the break-even line.} Here $pB=$ \twAebpB{} and $(1-p)K=$ \twAebqK, so by~\eqref{eq:line} \step{1}{4} pays if \twAebpB$\,\Delta s\,+$ \twAebqK$\,\Delta t>$ \twStepOneFourdC; with $\Delta s=0$ a specificity gain of \twStepOneFourdTBE{} suffices. The elicited $(\Delta s,\Delta t)=(\twStepOneFourdS,\twStepOneFourdT)$ returns \twStepOneFourdEVSI. The fence buyer weights both by \twAebLamVar{} and needs $\Delta J>\twDJStarAebOneFour$ (elicited \twDJAebOneFour).

\textbf{For the home robot, the gate turns on the prior against the threshold.}\label{sec:home} At the staged medians $p=\twHomeP$ and $\pist=\twHomePiStar$. After a negative reading from any rung but L7 the posterior stays above $\pist$: the agent delays anyway, and $\evsi=0$ on \twHomeAlwaysN{} of \twNHome{} rungs (Fig.~\ref{fig:gate}b). The L7 lab trials are in the gate but worth \twLSevenEVSI{} USD against a cost of \twLSevenC. So no launch-gate evaluation with a preset pass mark pays for itself here: nine rungs cannot change the decision, and L7 not by enough to repay its cost. No $(s,t)$ could make the pilot pay: $C(\mathrm{L9})=$ \twHomeCLNine{} $>\evpi=$ \twHomeEVPI.
At $\pist=\twHomePiStar$ every rung but L7 is closed toward delay for $p>\twHomeBandHiMax$ and toward launch for $p<\twHomeBandLoMin$ (gate bands in Table~\ref{tab:net}). Both $p$ and $\pist$ moved across elicitors: Opus alone gives $p=\twOpusHomeP$ but $\pist=\twOpusHomePiStar$, and \twOpusHomeGate{} rungs in the gate; at the pooled $s,t$ its prior with the pooled stakes puts \twOpusPriorOnlyGate{} rung in, its stakes with the pooled prior \twOpusStakesOnlyGate. Across prompts the prior moved: the same pair asked in one prompt gave $p=\twHomePSingleLo$ to $\twHomePSingleHi$ (at $p=\twCfP$, \twCfGate{} rungs are in the gate).
The closed gate is a property of the preset mark (Sec.~\ref{sec:model}), not a verdict that no evaluation is worth buying. The Gaussian-state elicitation (App.~\ref{app:gauss}) cannot isolate the mark: its home prior, \twGaussHomePLo{} to \twGaussHomePHi, already opens every gate, and at its plug-in point a preset-mark report and a continuous reading both exceed cost on the same \twGaussHomeStepfixPlug{} of \twNHome{} home rungs.

\textbf{Why stage the prompt} (finding iii). Under a single prompt the instrument text moved $p,B,K$ along a ladder: the coefficient of variation of the home $p$ across rungs was \twDriftCVp{} against \twDriftCVst{} for $s$ and $t$ (Haiku), \twDriftCVpSO{} against \twDriftCVsSO{} and \twDriftCVtSO{} (Sonnet+Opus). Staging removes this and lowered $C$ (App.~\ref{app:iterations}).

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Ask the gate question first, and the pass-mark question with it.} Fidelity matters only if some reading could change the decision~\citep{howard1966information}. Report each rung's gate band next to $p$ and $\pist$, and give a decision-maker outside the gate the continuous score. The home context quotes simulation violation rates that may raise $p$; eliciting $p$ under a second wording without them (about USD 14) is the first follow-up. A harm-rate state (e.g.\ injurious contacts per 1000 hours above a set rate) would make $p$ checkable; an injurious mode is close to certain for any current VLA.
\textbf{Sequential and complementary use.} Screening before a real confirmation~\citep{sinha2026rethinking,barrett2024benchmarkearly}, early stopping~\citep{snyder2025stopping} and fixing and re-testing failure modes give cheap rungs value our model sets to zero; pricing them needs the correlation between rungs' readings, which no safety study reports.
\textbf{Elicited inputs, and what would replace them.} Every input is a belief of one vendor's models, with no measured $s$, $t$ or $C$ and no human baseline (App.~\ref{app:limits}); paired sim-to-real numbers exist only for task success~\citep{li2024simpler,jain2025polaris,geminirobotics2025veo,majumdar2025predictive}. A paired study should test~\eqref{eq:line} on a step inside the decision's gate, such as AEB \step{1}{4}, with $n$ release candidates carrying a seeded pedestrian-braking fault and $n$ without. With independent errors the left side of~\eqref{eq:line} has standard deviation \twSettleSD$/\sqrt n$, so placing the break-even line two standard errors from the elicited value (\twSettleGap{} away) needs about \twSettleN{} candidates of each kind, against about \twSettleNFence{} for the fence criterion (\twSettleGapJ{} away); errors correlated at $\rho$ between the rungs cut $n$ by about $1-\rho$. Its cost is not elicited; its value is the sum of EVSI over the decisions it informs. Cheaper anchors: rating and claim data for AEB L8 and L9~\citep{iihs2024paeb,cicchino2022pedestrian}, a non-Claude elicitor (about USD 10 to 15), or 3 to 5 practitioners.

\section{Scenarios and context provenance}
\label{app:catalogue}

\textbf{Appendix map.} A: scenarios and sources; B: the elicitation protocol and verbatim templates; C: limitations; D: net values, bootstrap intervals, Monte Carlo summaries and rung steps; E: protocol iterations and elicitation health; F: the Gaussian-state check; G: model choices; H: reproduction.

Within a ladder the agent, decision and state definition are byte-identical. Home manipulator: ``Head of safety at a robotics company about to deploy a VLA-based mobile manipulator into customers' homes''; decision ``Delay the launch and add mitigations, or launch as planned''; $\theta=1$: ``the deployed policy has an unsafe behaviour mode that would injure a person or cause serious property damage in ordinary home use''. \section{Limitations}
\label{app:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Elicited, not measured.} Every input is the belief of one vendor's language models about a published evaluation described to them. The contexts were fact-checked, but the literature supplies almost no cost per run and no cross-rung false-negative rate for safety, so $s$, $t$ and $C$ are priors: in our catalogue \twNNoValidity{} of \twNRobotSafety{} safety-focused robotics evaluations report no validity evidence, and none of the \twNLevelFour{} closed-loop safety benchmarks reports a correlation with a real outcome. No rung is anchored to a measured sensitivity or specificity, there is no non-Claude elicitor, and nothing here is validated against human experts; Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion} lists the cheapest anchors.
\item \textbf{Round numbers.} \twRoundS{} of the Sonnet and Opus p50s of $s$ and \twRoundT{} of those of $t$ sit on a multiple of 0.05, and Sonnet answers $s=\twRoundModeS$ in \twRoundModeSN{} of its \twRoundModeSOf{} instrument elicitations, so the elicited $J$ takes few distinct values.
\item \textbf{Prompt and elicitor dependence.} The home gate verdict moves with the model set and with the prompt that elicited $p$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}); the staged prompt also lowered $C$. The home decision context quotes unsafe-episode and violation rates from closed-loop simulation benchmarks (App.~\ref{app:protocol}), evidence from the L4 class of instrument that the ladder prices. A second decision-context wording without those rates, with the same models, would bound how much of ``always respond'' follows from them; it has not been run.
\item \textbf{Decision model.} The action is binary and the state qualitative, so choosing and verifying mitigations, the main use of an evaluation once mitigation is expected, has no value; the graded-response Gaussian model values every rung (App.~\ref{app:gauss}). The model has no trial count per rung, no sim-to-real bias or correlation, and no combined use (a simulated screen followed by a real confirmation); rungs are substitutes.
\item \textbf{Point estimate.} The plug-in uses pooled medians. For one risk-neutral decision-maker uncertain about independent parameters the value is~\eqref{eq:evsi} at the parameters' predictive means (last column of Table~\ref{tab:net}), which depend on the fitted tails: $B$ and $K$ rise \twMeanRatioLo{} to \twMeanRatioHi{} times and the AEB EVPI from \twAebEVPI{} to \twAebEVPIMean, so the EVPI bound on fleet telemetry fails there, while the AEB order by net value (L8 first, L9 last) and the home verdict (every rung outside the gate, $\pist=\twHomePiStarMean$; $C(\mathrm{L9})=$ \twHomeCLNineMean{} $>\evpi=$ \twHomeEVPIMean) hold.
\item \textbf{Scope.} Two ladders; the AEB ladder lacks five rungs by design; home L8 and L9 are composites; the top rungs' $C$ is dominated by the exposure that rare-event rates need~\citep{kalra2016driving,zhao2019assessing}. Rank correlations over the \twNAeb{} AEB rungs cannot separate a moderate from a strong trend.
\item \textbf{Per-decision value and build-from-scratch cost.} No reuse count (App.~\ref{app:model}); one decision per ladder. The AEB ordering by net value cannot be overturned by any discount on the simulated rungs: even free, their EVSI (\twAebEVSILFour, \twAebEVSILSix) falls short of the track test's net value (\twAebNetLEight); the efficiency orderings and the home fence ordering can (L4's $\evsi^\star$, \twHomeEVSIStarLFour, exceeds L7's fence net value, \twHomeFenceNetLSeven).
\item \textbf{Statistics.} The Monte Carlo (MC) median is zero on every scenario (App.~\ref{app:summaries}); the bootstrap measures agreement between elicitations, not the width of each elicitation. The stored MC draws each rung's $p,B,K$ independently; only the step analysis shares draws along a ladder.
\item \textbf{Gaussian check.} The state variable is chosen by the elicitor and differs between repeats on some rungs; the derived $s,t$ disagree with the elicited ones.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Monte Carlo summaries.} An MC run draws \voiNDraws{} times from the mixture of the fitted distributions and reports the mean, the median, $P_+=P(\evsi>C)$ and $P_\mathrm{gate}=P(\evsi>0)$. The mixture spreads each rung across regimes: home rungs always respond on \twHomeAlwaysDrawsLo{} to \twHomeAlwaysDrawsHi\% of draws, and over all rungs $P_+$ is \twPposLo{} to \twPposHi{} and $P_\mathrm{gate}$ \twPgateLo{} to \twPgateHi, so the MC median EVSI is zero on all \voiMcZeroMedian{} scenarios and ranks nothing. The MC median has no decision-theoretic reading. The MC mean is the value to an agent who learns the parameters before deciding, a different question from the plug-in's; it agrees with the plug-in ranking at Spearman \voiPluginRhoMean{} but averages over a mass at zero. The fence ranking agrees with the plug-in where the plug-in is positive (Spearman \voiFenceRhoPlugin, $n=\voiFenceN$). $P_\mathrm{boot}(\mathrm{gate})$ (bootstrap of the medians) and $P_\mathrm{gate}$ (MC draws) answer different questions: the mixture pairs one repeat's stakes with another's prior and pulls every rung towards the middle. AEB L1 sits \twAebLOnePboot{} in the gate over the bootstrap because the prior lies just above its lower gate edge (\twAebBandLOneLo).

\textbf{Rung steps on common random numbers (CRN).} Drawing $p,B,K$ once per ladder and $s,t,C$ per rung, a step pays with probability \twCrnOneFour{} (AEB \step{1}{4}) and \twCrnEightNine{} (\step{8}{9}); the CRN median of $\Delta\evsi$ is zero on every step, so Table~\ref{tab:uplift} gives the plug-in steps only.

\textbf{p001 to p002.} The log-decade framing of the dollar questions reduced the cross-repeat spread of $B$ and $K$ only modestly over all 5 repeats ($B$ \voibaseNoiseB{} to \twPtwoNoiseB) and raised that of $C$; over the first 3 repeats all three fell ($B$ \twPoneNoiseBThree{} to \twPtwoNoiseBThree, $C$ \twPoneNoiseCThree{} to \twPtwoNoiseCThree). \section{The Gaussian-state family as a sanity check}
\label{app:gauss}

\textbf{What it asks.} The Gaussian protocol (g001) asks the elicitor to name a continuous state with a unit, its prior percentiles, the value $\theta_c$ at which the response changes, three routes to the sensor error relative to the prior spread $x$, four loss points, the stakes and $C$, under a linear-Gaussian sensor model. One reading removes a share $R^2=1/(1+x^2)$ of the prior variance of the state (the squared correlation between reading and state). Four action models are scored from the same answers: graded response with loss exponent $k$ (quad), binary action with linear payoff (lin), step payoff with a continuous reading (step), and step payoff with a pass/fail report at a fixed mark (stepfix), which maps one-to-one onto the binary model and alone has a gate. Notation: $d=(\mu_0-\theta_c)/\sigma_0$ is the standardised distance of the prior mean $\mu_0$ from $\theta_c$ ($\sigma_0$ the prior standard deviation), $\Phi$ the standard normal distribution function, so the implied prior is $p=\Phi(d)$, and $L$ the loss scale, the EVPI of the graded-response model, whose value is $LR^2$ for $k=2$. The stepfix mark sits at $\theta_c$.

\textbf{What it confirms.} The direction of the step efficiency: the MC median of eff$_\mathrm{step}$ correlates with level at $\twRhoGaussHome$ (home) and $\twRhoGaussAeb$ (AEB). The threshold-free rankings: the binary fence value agrees with the Gaussian fence-type values at \voiFenceStepfixRho{} to \voiFenceQuadRho{} under p004 (Table~\ref{tab:cmpplugin}, Fig.~\ref{fig:cmpplugin}) and at \voicmpsingleFenceStepfixRho{} to \voicmpsingleFenceQuadRho{} under p003 (the same comparison against the single-prompt run, not tabulated), and at \voibizFenceStepRho{} to \voibizFenceStepfixRho{} over \voibizGaussN{} decisions of a separate study from other domains, elicited the same way (Sonnet+Opus; binary plug-in against stepfix \voibizGaussPluginRhoStepfix{} there).

\textbf{The home gate.} The Gaussian run's implied home prior $\Phi(d)$ is \twGaussHomePLo{} to \twGaussHomePHi, below the upper gate edges of the binary run, and the stepfix value is positive on every rung. At the Gaussian plug-in point stepfix and step both exceed cost on the same \twGaussHomeStepfixPlug{} of \twNHome{} home rungs; over the MC draws the median exceeds cost on \twGaussHomeStepfixMC{} rungs for stepfix and \twGaussHomeStepMC{} for step. Within this family the preset mark lowers the value of a reading but does not close a gate at these priors, so the family cannot show how much of the binary verdict is the mark.

\textbf{Where it disagrees.} The gated plug-in: $\voiGaussPluginRhoStepfix$ to $\voiGaussPluginRhoQuad$ against the staged binary run, because the binary plug-in is zero on the \twHomeAlwaysN{} always-respond home rungs while every Gaussian model is positive; against the single-prompt run, where every rung is in the gate, it is \voicmpsingleGaussPluginRhoKg{} to \voicmpsingleGaussPluginRhoQuad. The implied instrument: the derived $s$ (\twGaussSDerLo{} to \twGaussSDerHi) and $t$ (\twGaussTDerLo{} to \twGaussTDerHi) are nearly flat across rungs and sit above the elicited ones (mean difference \voiGaussBiasS{} and \voiGaussBiasT; Spearman \voiGaussRhoS{} and \voiGaussRhoT). The derived prior has the smallest mean difference ($\voiGaussBiasP$), but its median absolute difference (\voiGaussMadP) exceeds that of $t$ (\voiGaussMadT), and because the elicited $p$ takes one value per ladder, its Spearman $\rho=\voiGaussRhoP$ mostly measures the separation of the two ladders (Fig.~\ref{fig:derived}). $R^2$ sits at \twGaussRtwoLo{} to \twGaussRtwoHi{} on every rung and falls with the rung on the home ladder (Spearman $\twGaussRtwoRhoHome$), so the Gaussian ranking is set by the loss scale over cost, as the binary one is set by cost. \textbf{Caveats.} The elicitor chooses the state variable, and on \twGaussUnitRungs{} rungs the repeats pool physically different states (a rate with a contact force or an impact speed); \twGaussFitFlags{} parameter fits in \twGaussFitElic{} elicitations carry a fit warning over all models (\voigaussFitWarnings{} for Sonnet+Opus).

\section{Model choice}
\label{app:model}

\textbf{Why the efficacy multiplier $e$ was dropped.} An earlier version multiplied $B$ by $e$, the probability that the agent acts on the signal and that the action works. It conflated compliance (part of the decision rule) with efficacy (part of $B$), discounted the benefit branch but not the false-alarm branch, and barely mattered: over the \twETopQuartileN{} top-quartile decisions of a separate study its mean $|\rho|$ against efficiency was \twEGlobal{} against \twEGlobalC{} for $C$, and dropping it left the ranking of all \voibizGaussN{} decisions in place (Spearman \twERho).

\textbf{Why $s$ and $t$ stay.} They create the gate: every zero in Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder} is theirs. Dropping them collapses the ranking to $\evpi/C$; at the headline plug-in point that ranking agrees with $\evsi/C$ at \twSimpRhoGate{} inside the gate but only \twSimpRhoAll{} over all rungs. Along a ladder they are, with $C$, the only inputs that differ by design; the fence value is linear in Youden's index and, inside the gate, the decision's own value is linear in $s$ and $t$~\eqref{eq:line}.

\textbf{What to add next: the reuse count.} With $C$ defined as build plus one run, a simulation suite built once and run on every release is charged its whole build cost for each decision. For an evaluation that informs $n$ decisions the net value is $n\,\evsi-C_\mathrm{build}-n\,C_\mathrm{run}$; $n=1$ reproduces the present objective. $C_\mathrm{build}$, $C_\mathrm{run}$ and $n$ belong to the instrument stage of the staged protocol, next to $s$ and $t$. Per decision a rung can gain at most its $C$ from reuse, so on the AEB ladder, where the simulated rungs' EVSI is below the track test's net value, no reuse count changes the first place; on the efficiency orderings and on the home fence ordering it could, and eliciting $n$ would tell.

\section{Reproduction}
