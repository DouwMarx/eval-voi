\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\emph{Decision model.} A developer of an LLM or of a physical-AI system faces one release decision (Fig.~\ref{fig:method}): mitigate (delay the release and reduce the hazard) or deploy as planned. A hidden binary state $\theta$ is 1 when the hazard is present, so that mitigating is correct; the developer's prior belief that it is present is $p$. We model the evaluation as a measurement instrument for $\theta$: one run returns a positive or negative result $x$ with sensitivity $s=P(x{=}1\mid\theta{=}1)$ and specificity $t=P(x{=}0\mid\theta{=}0)$, which measure its construct validity~\citep{eriksson2025trust}, how well the score tracks the hazard. A false negative: a model passes a virology question set yet gives real uplift; a false positive: a vehicle fails a track test yet would brake on the road. Mitigating gains $B$ when the hazard is present (the harm avoided) and loses $K$ when it is absent (the benefit forgone), both valued for society, in USD, for this one release. With stakes $\Lambda=B+K$ the developer should mitigate once the belief exceeds $\pist=K/\Lambda$~\citep{pauker1980threshold}; acting on a belief $\pi$ is worth $V(\pi)=\Lambda\,[\pi-\pist]^+$ over deploying, the result moves the belief by Bayes' rule to $\pi_x$, and the value of one run and its value per dollar (its information efficiency) are
\begin{equation}
\EVSI = \mathbb{E}_x[V(\pi_x)]-V(p), \qquad \eta=\EVSI/C, \qquad C=\Cb+\Cr,
\label{eq:evsi}
\end{equation}
where $\Cb$ is the cost to build the evaluation and $\Cr$ the cost of one run. We rank by value per dollar because funding evaluations in that order until a finite budget is spent buys the most safety, the greedy solution of the knapsack problem~\citep{dantzig1957knapsack}. EVSI is zero when the prior is already decisive, that is, when no result would change the decision. Since the elicited prior describes today's beliefs, we also report the maximum of EVSI over the threshold at fixed stakes, reached when the developer is exactly undecided ($\pist=p$):
\begin{equation}
\EVSIst=\Lambda\,p(1-p)\,(s+t-1), \qquad \etast=\EVSIst/C,
\label{eq:evsistar}
\end{equation}
stakes times the variance of the state times Youden's index $J=s+t-1$~\citep{youden1950index}. $\EVSIst$, the value to a still undecided decision maker such as the builder of an evaluation, is positive for every informative evaluation, so $\etast$ ranks all of them; the build is repaid after $n^*=\Cb/(\EVSI-\Cr)$ runs.

\emph{Evaluations.} The \voiNLLM{} LLM safety evaluations are those LLM system cards report, ranked before any result was seen by how often system cards report them and how directly their score can be checked against the hazard, from five risk domains (cyber, chemical and biological, loss of control, harmful manipulation and societal harm). The \voiNPhysical{} physical-AI safety evaluations are published evaluations of embodied systems that score the rate of a physically harmful event, chosen to span the fidelity levels of the sim-to-real gap from text-only planning to track testing. Each evaluation gets one release decision written from its own sources, a definition of $\theta$, and two paragraphs of sourced facts (decision, evaluation) curated from cached primary sources and fact-checked by language-model agents (Claude Fable 5.1); the prompts are released with the code.

\emph{Elicitation.} An ensemble of LLMs serves as a cost-efficient expert panel: LLMs have elicited priors before~\citep{capstick2024autoelicit,selby2025hadenough}, following expert-elicitation practice~\citep{ohagan2006uncertain}, and a diverse LLM crowd forecasts about as well as a human one~\citep{schoenegger2024wisdom}. The stakes and the instrument are elicited independently: a decision prompt asks for $p$, $B$ and $K$ and never describes the evaluation; an instrument prompt asks for $s$, $t$, $\Cb$ and $\Cr$ and never states the stakes. For each parameter the member writes its reasoning, then the 5th and 95th percentiles as values it would be surprised to see the truth fall outside, then its median. The members are \voiNMembers{} models from \voiNMembers{} developers (\voiMemberList), each answering each prompt once; of \voiAttempts{} answers, \voiValidShare{} were valid after one retry. % CHECK-DATA: members and repeats of the analysed run

\emph{Monte Carlo and comparison.} Each answer's three percentiles are fitted with a Beta distribution ($p$, $s$, $t$) or a lognormal ($B$, $K$, $\Cb$, $\Cr$). On each of \voiDraws{} draws, every parameter of the evaluation under study is sampled from the fitted distribution of one member chosen at random with equal chance (the linear opinion pool~\citep{stone1961opinionpool}). All evaluations are ranked on every draw, which gives each a distribution of ranks and the group comparison an interval; where a single estimate is needed, every parameter is set to its median across the members. The probability of superiority $\PS$ is the probability that a randomly chosen physical-AI evaluation, among those tested, has a higher $\etast$ than a randomly chosen LLM evaluation, the Mann-Whitney $U$ statistic over the number of pairs~\citep{mcgraw1992cles}; we report its median and 90\% interval over the draws, and an exact Mann-Whitney test at the single estimate gives $p_\mathrm{MW}$. Under $\eta$, evaluations worth zero tie and count as one half.
