\emph{Decision model.} In this model a decision maker, the developer of an LLM or of a physical-AI system, faces one release decision (Fig.~\ref{fig:method}, an influence diagram): mitigate (delay the release and reduce the hazard) or deploy as planned. A hidden binary state $\theta$ is 1 when the hazard is present, so that mitigating is correct; the decision maker's prior belief that it is present is $p$. The evaluation is a measurement instrument for $\theta$: one run returns a positive or negative result $x$ with sensitivity $s=P(x{=}1\mid\theta{=}1)$ and specificity $t=P(x{=}0\mid\theta{=}0)$, which measure how representative its result is of the underlying hazard, a form of construct validity~\citep{eriksson2025trust}. Mitigating gains $B$ when the hazard is present (the harm avoided) and loses $K$ when it is absent (the benefit forgone), both valued for society, in USD, for this one release. With stakes $\Lambda=B+K$ the decision maker should mitigate once the belief exceeds $\pist=K/\Lambda$~\citep{pauker1980threshold}; acting on a belief $\pi$ is worth $V(\pi)=\Lambda\,[\pi-\pist]^+$ relative to deploying as planned, where $[z]^+=\max(z,0)$; the result moves the belief by Bayes' rule to $\pi_x$, and the expected value of sample information of the evaluation and its value per dollar are
\begin{equation}
\EVSI = \mathbb{E}_x[V(\pi_x)]-V(p), \qquad \eta=\EVSI/C, \qquad C=\Cb+\Cr,
\label{eq:evsi}
\end{equation}
where $\Cb$ is the cost to build the evaluation and $\Cr$ the cost of one run. We rank by $\eta$ because funding evaluations in that order until a finite budget is spent is the greedy rule of the knapsack problem~\citep{dantzig1957knapsack}, optimal when the budget can be split across evaluations and near-optimal otherwise. Note that EVSI is zero when the prior is already decisive, so that no result would change the decision. Since the elicited prior describes beliefs as of October 2026, we also report the maximum of EVSI over the threshold at fixed stakes, reached when the decision maker is exactly undecided ($\pist=p$),
\begin{equation}
\EVSIst=\Lambda\,p(1-p)\,|s+t-1|, \qquad \etast=\EVSIst/C,
\label{eq:evsistar}
\end{equation}
which is positive for every informative evaluation, so $\etast$ ranks all of them. The build is repaid after $n^*=\Cb/(\EVSI-\Cr)$ runs.

\emph{Evaluations.} The \voiNLLM{} LLM safety evaluations are those LLM system cards report most, from five risk domains: cyber~\voiCiteCyber; chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear (CBRN)~\voiCiteCBRN; loss of control~\voiCiteLossOfControl; harmful manipulation~\voiCiteHarmfulManipulation; societal harm~\voiCiteSocietalHarm. The \voiNPhysical{} physical-AI safety evaluations~\voiCitePhysical{} are published evaluations of embodied systems that measure the rate of a physically harmful event, chosen to span the fidelity levels of the sim-to-real gap from text-only planning to a real-world track test. The reference numbers are the evaluation ids of Fig.~\ref{fig:rows}. Each evaluation has one release decision written from its own sources, a definition of $\theta$, and two paragraphs of sourced facts (decision, evaluation) extracted from cached primary sources by Claude Fable 5.1 agents.

\emph{Elicitation.} An ensemble of LLMs serves as a cost-efficient expert panel: LLMs have elicited priors before~\citep{capstick2024autoelicit,selby2025hadenough}, following expert-elicitation practice~\citep{ohagan2006uncertain}, and a diverse LLM crowd forecasts about as well as a human one~\citep{schoenegger2024wisdom}. The stakes and the instrument are elicited independently: a decision prompt asks for $p$, $B$ and $K$ without describing the evaluation, an instrument prompt for $s$, $t$, $\Cb$ and $\Cr$ without stating the stakes. For each parameter the member writes its reasoning, then its 5th and 95th percentiles, then its median. The \voiNMembers{} members (\voiMemberList) come from \voiNMembers{} different developers and each answers each prompt once; of \voiAttempts{} attempts, \voiValidShare{} were valid. % CHECK-DATA: members and repeats of the analysed run

\emph{Monte Carlo and comparison.} Each answer's three percentiles are fitted with a Beta ($p$, $s$, $t$) or lognormal ($B$, $K$, $\Cb$, $\Cr$) distribution; on each of \voiDraws{} draws every parameter of the evaluation under study is sampled from the fitted distribution of a member chosen at random (the linear opinion pool~\citep{stone1961opinionpool}), and all evaluations are ranked. The probability of superiority $\PSst$ is the probability that a randomly chosen physical-AI evaluation, among those tested, has a higher $\etast$ than a randomly chosen LLM evaluation, the Mann-Whitney $U$ statistic over the number of pairs~\citep{mcgraw1992cles}; $\PS$ is the same under $\eta$, where evaluations worth zero tie and count as one half.
