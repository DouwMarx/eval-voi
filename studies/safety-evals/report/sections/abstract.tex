\begin{abstract}
A safety evaluation is worth money when its result can change a decision and thereby avoid harm.
Physical-AI safety evaluations look expensive next to many in-silico large language model (LLM) safety evaluations.
We rank evaluations by their expected value of sample information (EVSI) per unit cost, using a simple decision model whose parameters are elicited as probability distributions from an ensemble of \voiNMembers{} LLMs for \voiNPhysical{} physical-AI and \voiNLLM{} LLM safety evaluations. EVSI and the other metrics are estimated by Monte Carlo sampling.
Among the evaluations tested, a randomly chosen physical-AI evaluation gives an undecided decision maker more value of information per unit cost than a randomly chosen LLM evaluation only about \voiEtaStarPSQMed{} of the time, although the best physical-AI evaluation places in the top half of all evaluations on \voiEtaStarPBestPhysicalTopHalf{} of draws. % CHECK-DATA
In both groups, evaluations repay their build within a few runs, depending on how certain the decision maker is about the hazard beforehand. % CHECK-DATA
The value of information per unit cost of physical-AI and LLM safety evaluations differs mainly in the stakes of the decision, estimated at about \voiStakesRatio{} times higher for an LLM release, a gap that can close with wider robot deployment. % CHECK-DATA
\end{abstract}
