\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\emph{Group comparison.} Our results show that a randomly chosen physical-AI evaluation has a higher $\etast$ than a randomly chosen LLM evaluation with probability $\PS=\voiEtaStarPSQMed$ (90\% interval \voiEtaStarPSQLo{} to \voiEtaStarPSQHi); under $\eta$, where the result must be able to change the decision and about half of the evaluations of both groups are worth zero, $\PS=\voiEtaPSQMed$ (\voiEtaPSQLo{} to \voiEtaPSQHi), an interval that contains one half, so the groups cannot be told apart. % CHECK-DATA
So among the evaluations tested, a physical-AI safety evaluation is unlikely to be the better value for an undecided developer, yet the best physical-AI evaluation ranks in the top half of all \voiNScenarios{} on \voiEtaStarPBestPhysicalTopHalf{} of draws and in the top 10 on \voiEtaStarPBestPhysicalTopTen{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:rows}). Ordered by the median rank of their evaluations by $\etast$, best first, the risk domains run \voiDomainOrder\ifextended{} (Table~\ref{tab:domains})\fi. % CHECK-DATA

\emph{Why the groups differ.} Physical-AI and LLM evaluations have similar medians of sensitivity and specificity ($s$ \voiMedSPhysical{} against \voiMedSLLM, $t$ \voiMedTPhysical{} against \voiMedTLLM) and of cost ($C$ \voiMedCPhysical{} against \voiMedCLLM). The decisions differ: the median stakes $B+K$ are \voiMedStakesLLM{} for an LLM release against \voiMedStakesPhysical{} for a physical-AI release, a factor of \voiStakesRatio, and $\EVSIst$ scales with the stakes (Fig.~\ref{fig:headline}a). % CHECK-DATA

\emph{Zeros and pay-back.} With every parameter at its median, the prior is already decisive for \voiZeroSharePhysical{} of the physical-AI and \voiZeroShareLLM{} of the LLM evaluations (Fig.~\ref{fig:headline}b), so in our model the evaluation has no value: the developer should mitigate (or deploy) whatever the result. Where the result can change the decision, one run is worth a median of \voiMedEVSIChangingPhysical{} for a physical-AI and \voiMedEVSIChangingLLM{} for an LLM evaluation, against the groups' median build costs of \voiMedCbuildPhysical{} and \voiMedCbuildLLM, so these evaluations repay their build after a median of \voiNstarMedianPhysical{} runs (physical AI) and \voiNstarMedianLLM{} runs (LLM); over the draws on which one run is worth more than its run cost, the median break-even count is \voiNstarDrawMedianPhysical{} and \voiNstarDrawMedianLLM{} runs (Fig.~\ref{fig:rows}, right). % CHECK-DATA

\emph{Fidelity.} Across the \voiNLevel{} physical-AI evaluations (fidelity levels \voiLevelMin{} to \voiLevelMax), fidelity level shows no detectable association with value per dollar: Spearman correlation $\voiLevelRhoEtaStar$ with $\etast$ ($p=\voiLevelRhoEtaStarp$) and $\voiLevelRhoEta$ with $\eta$ ($p=\voiLevelRhoEtap$). % CHECK-DATA

Over the draws, $\etast$ co-varies most with \voiRhoIndTopParam{} and \voiRhoIndSecondParam{} and least with \voiRhoIndLowParam{} (mean absolute Spearman \voiRhoIndTopValue, \voiRhoIndSecondValue{} and \voiRhoIndLowValue): the build cost moves an evaluation's own value, but the groups' costs are alike, so cost does not separate them. % CHECK-DATA
