\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\emph{Group comparison.} Over the draws, a randomly chosen physical-AI evaluation has a higher $\etast$ than a randomly chosen LLM evaluation with probability $\PS=\voiEtaStarPSQMed$ (90\% interval \voiEtaStarPSQLo{} to \voiEtaStarPSQHi) (exact Mann-Whitney $p_\mathrm{MW}=\voiEtaStarMWp$); under $\eta$, where the elicited prior counts and most evaluations of both groups tie at zero, $\PS=\voiEtaPSQMed$ (\voiEtaPSQLo{} to \voiEtaPSQHi). % CHECK-DATA
So among the evaluations tested, a physical-AI safety evaluation is unlikely to be the better value for money, and the best one is not close: it ranks \voiEtaStarBestPhysicalRankCentral{} of \voiNScenarios{} by $\etast$ at the median parameters and reaches the top 10 on \voiEtaStarPBestPhysicalTopTen{} of draws and the top 5 on \voiEtaStarPBestPhysicalTopFive{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:rows}). By risk domain, the median rank by $\etast$ runs \voiDomainOrder\ifextended{} (Table~\ref{tab:domains})\fi. % CHECK-DATA

\emph{Why the groups differ.} Sensitivity, specificity and cost have similar medians in the two groups\ifextended{} (physical AI against LLM: $s$ \voiMedSPhysical{} and \voiMedSLLM, $t$ \voiMedTPhysical{} and \voiMedTLLM, $C$ \voiMedCPhysical{} and \voiMedCLLM)\fi. The decisions differ: the median stakes $B+K$ are \voiMedStakesLLM{} for an LLM release against \voiMedStakesPhysical{} for a physical-AI release, a factor of \voiStakesRatio, and $\EVSIst$ scales with the stakes. Over the draws, $\etast$ co-varies most with \voiRhoIndTopParam{} and \voiRhoIndSecondParam{} and least with \voiRhoIndLowParam{} (mean absolute Spearman \voiRhoIndTopValue, \voiRhoIndSecondValue{} and \voiRhoIndLowValue): the build cost moves an evaluation's own value, but the groups' costs are alike, so cost does not separate them. % CHECK-DATA

\emph{Zeros and pay-back.} With every parameter at its median, the prior is already decisive for \voiZeroSharePhysical{} of the physical-AI and \voiZeroShareLLM{} of the LLM evaluations: the developer should mitigate (or deploy) whatever the result. On the draws where one run is worth more than its run cost (\voiPNstarFiniteMedianPhysical{} of draws for the median physical-AI evaluation, \voiPNstarFiniteMedianLLM{} for the median LLM one), the build is repaid after a median of \voiNstarDrawMedianPhysical{} runs (physical AI) and \voiNstarDrawMedianLLM{} runs (LLM) (below one: the first run repays the build; Fig.~\ref{fig:rows}, right). % CHECK-DATA

\emph{Rank stability and fidelity.} The draws carry the elicitors' disagreement into the ranks: the median interquartile range of an evaluation's rank by $\etast$ is \voiEtaStarRankIQRMedian{} of \voiNScenarios{} places, so single ranks are not settled while the group ordering is. Across the \voiNLevel{} physical-AI evaluations (fidelity levels \voiLevelMin{} to \voiLevelMax), fidelity level and $\etast$ have Spearman correlation $\voiLevelRhoEtaStar$ ($p=\voiLevelRhoEtaStarp$): higher fidelity does not buy more value per dollar in this set. % CHECK-DATA
