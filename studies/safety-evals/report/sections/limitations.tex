\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:limitations}

Every parameter is an LLM's belief. Despite the precautions (\voiNMembers{} models from \voiNMembers{} developers, none of them the developer of the fact-curating model; the same sourced facts for every member), the parameters are not validated against human experts, LLM intervals are often too narrow~\citep{hobor2026bayesian}, and no evaluation publishes a sensitivity and specificity against real harm. Each decision is written from the evaluation's own sources, so the comparison is between the decisions as much as between the evaluations; pricing both groups against one shared decision would separate the two, and the groups are not matched pairs. Published physical-AI safety evaluations are few and uneven (most of the \voiNPhysical{} put a language model inside a robot), so the result also describes today's evaluations.

\label{sec:conclusion}
In sum, physical-AI safety evaluations are worth less per dollar than LLM safety evaluations because far less is at stake per release, not because they cost more or measure worse; even the best of them rarely reaches the top ten, and the gap closes only as physical-AI stakes grow.
