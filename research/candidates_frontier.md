## Sources and method

- Corpus counts: system-card corpus docs.sqlite, 187 documents with safety_evals=1 from 22 publishers (DB last_checked 2026-10-01), regex query over normalised text (whitespace collapsed, end-of-line hyphenation joined, as in research/mine_system_cards.py), run 2026-10-01. The regex per candidate is in the JSON (`corpus.query`); the regex per excluded row with a count is in `excluded[].corpus.query`. Counts differ by one or two documents from research/system_card_evals.md because the corpus grew from 180 to 187 documents since that file was written.
- Primary sources: fetched on 2026-10-01 through the arXiv API (title, abstract, date) and, for cost and validity facts, the full text at arxiv.org/html or the publisher page named in each entry. Developer-private evaluations have the system card as their only primary source; those are cited by corpus slug with the canonical URL in the JSON.
- Start set: the ten pilot evaluations in studies/ai-safety-evals/scenarios.json (marked pilot). Candidates added from the top 60 in research/system_card_evals.md and from corpus names with at least two publishers or ten documents.

## Ranking rule

Score = presence + source + hazard + instrument + penalties (maximum 17).

- presence: min(publishers naming it, 8) + min(documents naming it, 24)/12; counts from the corpus query stated per candidate
- source: 3 public instrument (data and code released); 2.5 public set plus private official set; 2 public paper or report with private or on-request task data; 1 developer or third-party report stating task counts and scoring only; 0 none
- hazard: 2 programmatic or expert-baselined score; 1.5 LLM-judge score with reported human agreement >= 0.8; 1 judge score with partial validation (jury consensus, self-consistency) or a public classifier without reported agreement; 0.5 judge without validation; 0 not checkable
- instrument: 2 single build-and-run instrument; 1 suite or tool that a default configuration fixes to one instrument; 0 behaviour, organisation, programme or human trial
- penalties: -1 each: saturated at the frontier; documented construct problem (shortcuts, contamination, scorer gaming, evaluation awareness, refusal confound); developer-private; no standalone primary source; capability-origin proxy. -2 human-subject trial

## Inclusion threshold

include iff instrument >= 1 and source >= 1 and hazard >= 1 and (publishers >= 2 or documents >= 10) and total >= 8.0; floor 8.0 equals a public single instrument with an objective score named by two publishers after one fit penalty (2 + 0.3 + 3 + 2 + 2 - 1). Domain floor: a domain with fewer than three included candidates admits its best remaining candidate that passes every hard criterion, flagged borderline.

25 of 40 candidates pass the rule. Domain-floor admissions: DeceptionBench (harmful_manipulation had two included candidates; it passes every hard criterion at 7.75, flagged borderline). 26 included.

Per domain: cbrn 6; cyber 8; loss_of_control 4; harmful_manipulation 3 (one by the domain floor); societal_harm 5.

Named in 3 corpus documents by 2 publishers (anthropic, meta). Primary source: 2510.15501 (public: https://github.com/Aries-iai/DeceptionBench (stated in abstract)); public. Single instrument: yes, fixed scenario set with judge scoring. Decision: Delay the consumer launch and add training against assisting deception vs launch on schedule. theta = 1: the model helps users deceive third parties in a material fraction of scenarios. Cost: no published cost fact. Validity: "The results demonstrate robust inter-annotator agreement with an exact match rate of 95.7% (all three annotators concurring), and our automated evaluator achieves 97.1% alignment with human majority votes." [2510.15501]; "To prevent potential bias in using GPT-4o as the evaluator, we conduct human validation on a representative subset of 420 interaction records (10 per model per inducement level)." [2510.15501]; "Muse Spark 1.1 shows elevated verbalized evaluation awareness on DeceptionBench, which raises questions about the generalizability of these results" [meta-muse-spark-1-1-system-card (meta, 2026-07-09)]. Fit problems: elevated evaluation awareness reported by Meta; two publishers, three documents; human validation covers 420 of the 1,000+ records, scored by GPT-4o. Score 7.75 = presence 2.25 + source 3 + hazard 1.5 + instrument 2, penalties construct -1. Admitted by the domain floor for harmful_manipulation; borderline.

## Notes for the orchestrator

- Harmful manipulation is thin: only MASK and Petri pass the rule; DeceptionBench is admitted by the domain floor (hazard 1.5: the paper reports 95.7% three-annotator exact agreement and 97.1% evaluator agreement with the human majority on a 420-record subset, arXiv 2510.15501; total 7.75, borderline). Labs measure manipulation as behaviours (sycophancy 29 docs/9 publishers) and with audit tools, not with shared instruments.
- Cyber dominates the top of the ranking because public, programmatically scored instruments exist there and most publishers run the same ones; 4 of the 8 included cyber evaluations carry a saturation penalty (CyberGym, Cybench, CVE-Bench, ExploitBench) and are kept for the ceiling-effect comparison the pilot rationale asked for.
- Cost facts: 9 of 26 included candidates carry a dollar figure (run cost per task or per run, prize money, or per-item author payments); 12 more carry only run-size facts (task counts, epochs, token budgets); DeceptionBench carries none; the only build-cost figure in the whole set is WMDP's 'over $200K'. Validity facts exist for 26 of 26, as judge agreement, expert baselines or documented failure modes; no candidate has a measured link from score to realised harm.
- LAB-Bench is included with a capability-origin penalty because six to seven publishers use its ProtocolQA and CloningScenarios subsets as the biology-uplift proxy; drop it if the study wants only purpose-built hazard instruments.
