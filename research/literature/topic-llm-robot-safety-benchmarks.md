# LLM / VLM robot safety benchmarks: literature catalogue

Generated 2026-09-28. 91 entries, 91 with fetched URLs. Companion JSON: `topic-llm-robot-safety-benchmarks.json`. Levels follow the task's 0-9 sim-to-real ladder (not the levels in the sibling speaker files).

## Synthesis (one page)

**What exists.** This catalogue holds 91 verified entries (86 evaluations, tools or datasets plus 5 surveys or position papers), almost all from 2024-2026, in four families:

1. Offline QA benchmarks scored against human labels (ladder 0-3): ASIMOV v1/v2, SciFi-Benchmark, MSSBench, EARBench, SafeMind, EmbodyGuard, GuardianBench, EgoSafetyBench, ROBOSHACKLES. Input is text, an edited/generated image, or a generated video plus a candidate action; output is a safe/unsafe verdict or an intervention flag. Sizes range from ~300 (ASIMOV-2.0 tracks) to 5,558 (SafeMind) items, with millions of auto-generated training items behind ASIMOV.
2. Closed-loop simulation benchmarks (ladder 4): SafeAgentBench (750 tasks, AI2-THOR), IS-Bench (161 scenarios / 388 risks, OmniGibson), Safe-BeAl (2,027 tasks, VirtualHome), HomeSafeBench (1,000 tasks), SafetyALFRED, SENTINEL, and the 2026 wave of VLA-level suites (ForesightSafety-VLA, SafeVLA-Bench, LIBERO-Safety, HazardArena, SafeManip, MANIGUARD, SafeStage, OopsieVerse). Scoring is either an LLM judge on the plan or simulator predicates / temporal-logic monitors on the trajectory.
3. Red-teaming and attack studies (ladder 4-7): RoboPAIR, BadRobot, POEX, Blindfold, ANNIE, AttackVLA, FreezeVLA, JailWAM, environmental and physical prompt injection. Metric is attack success rate; several execute on real arms or quadrupeds.
4. Runtime guards and tooling evaluated as safety filters (ladder 4-7): RoboGuard, SAFER, CORE, RoboSafe, SafeGate, AESOP, semantic safety filters, HomeGuard, VLESA.

**Where cost evidence is strong or absent.** Almost no paper reports dollars or person-hours. The usable cost signals are: IS-Bench (2.5-3 h per evaluation session on one A100), SafeAgentBench (CPU plus one RTX 4090; 1,008 human ratings for judge validation), RoboGuard (12-21% of attacker tokens, 1 vs 15 LLM queries), RoboPAIR (max 10 queries per attack, 35 real trials per robot, 2 expert annotators), BadRobot (35 real trials per attack on two low-cost arms), ASIMOV-2.0 (5 raters per item), Veo simulator and Predictive Red Teaming (1,600+ and 500+ real-robot trials spent to validate a proxy), ResponsibleRobotBench (an internal cost model: human intervention = 100 actions). Everything at ladder 4 is implicitly cheap (simulator only); everything at ladder 7 is a few dozen trials. No entry sits at ladder 8 or 9 for LLM/VLM safety; the only operational-data entry (InspecSafe-V1) is a static replay dataset.

**Where validity evidence is strong or absent.** Human agreement is reported by a minority: SafeAgentBench (GPT-4o judge vs human 90.4-91.9%), EARBench (judge kappa 0.48 without and 0.85 with safety tips), EgoSafetyBench (inter-annotator kappa 0.744 chunk / 0.804 video, plus explicit false-alarm rates 16-37%), HomeSafeBench (human ceiling 98% F1 vs best model 34.7%), PhysicalJailbreakBench (false-positive rates 11.7-13.7%). Sim-to-real predictive validity is quantified only by the Google DeepMind proxy studies: Veo world simulator (Pearson 0.86, MMRV 0.06 vs real OOD success) and Predictive Red Teaming (<0.19 gap vs 500+ hardware trials), neither of which targets hazard behaviour specifically. No entry links a benchmark score to real incident or injury outcomes; ASIMOV-2.0 and ICAT are grounded in real injury/incident reports on the input side only. The recurring failure mode that a VOI analysis must model is the success-safety gap: 13-56% of successful VLA rollouts violate a safety spec (SafeVLA-Bench, MANIGUARD, ForesightSafety-VLA), and refusal-style evals over-trigger (EgoSafetyBench, HomeGuard, Semantic DoS).

**Canonical entries for a 4-page paper.** (1) `sermanet2025asimov` and (2) `jindal2025danger`: the injury-report-grounded, human-labelled QA line with the largest scale and ISO-derived constraints. (3) `yin2024safeagentbench`: closed-loop planning benchmark with a measured judge-vs-human agreement. (4) `lu2025isbench`: process-level interactive safety with an explicit wall-clock cost. (5) `robey2024robopair` and (6) `zhang2024badrobot`: real-robot jailbreak studies with trial counts and near-100% attack success. (7) `geminirobotics2025veo` (with `majumdar2025predictive`): the only quantified proxy-vs-real validity numbers. (8) `panpatil2026egosafetybench`: the best-documented labelling reliability and sensitivity/false-alarm trade-off. Supporting framing: `wan2026nofreechecker` (verifier cost vs credibility) and `kim2026modularguardrails` (position).

## Method and caveats

- Sources: 8 rounds of search (WebSearch until the session budget ran out, then the arXiv export API), every kept URL fetched (arXiv abstract page; HTML full text for the core seeds; ASIMOV-2.0 project page). One API-listing hallucination ('HazardBench 2605.06781') was caught by fetching the id (it is an astrophysics paper) and dropped.
- Saturation: rounds 7 and 8 added three and one relevant items respectively; the sweep stopped at practical saturation rather than two fully empty rounds.
- `org` is given only where the abstract page or prior knowledge makes it clear; 'not stated on abstract page' otherwise. Affiliations are not in arXiv abstract metadata.
- `sim2real_level` is the highest ladder level at which the evaluation is actually scored; notes give secondary levels (e.g., ASIMOV injury text at 0).
- Numbers marked 'not in abstract' were not extracted from full text; confidence is lowered accordingly.
- Out of scope but adjacent: autonomous-driving VLA safety suites, Safety-Gymnasium-style RL benchmarks, non-LLM collision-avoidance filters, and the Gemini Robotics 2 safety report (ASIMOV-Agentic) which was not fetched in this session (see speaker-sindhwani.json).
