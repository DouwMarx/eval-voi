# Adversarial attacks and red teaming of embodied AI: evaluation catalogue

Scope: 2018-2026, jailbreaks of LLM/VLM robot planners, adversarial and backdoor attacks on VLA and visuomotor policies, automated red teaming (instruction, scene, geometry), generative (LLM/diffusion) adversarial scenario generation for driving, physical-world attacks on ADAS/AV perception, plus the surveys, standards and position papers that frame them. 56 entries, all primary URLs fetched. Companion JSON: `topic-adversarial-red-teaming.json`.

## One-page synthesis

What exists
- Three attack families dominate: (1) text/voice jailbreaks of LLM planners (RoboPAIR, BadRobot, RoboGCG), (2) visual attacks on VLAs and diffusion policies (patches, freezing, natural diffusion patches, 3D objects), (3) training-time backdoors (GoBA, DropVLA, BackdoorVLA, State Backdoor, TrojanRobot, MuTRAP). Reported attack success rates (ASR) are routinely 90-100 percent in simulation for white-box attacks.
- Automated red teaming that searches over inputs rather than attacking one model: instruction paraphrase (ERT, DAERT), scene and risk-object placement (RedVLA), object geometry (Geometric Red-Teaming), generated off-nominal images (Predictive Red Teaming), search-based test generation (FATE-VLA). These are the closest analogues to a reusable "evaluation" rather than a one-off exploit.
- Planner-safety benchmarks (SafeAgentBench, IS-Bench, EARBench, MSSBench, RoboJailBench) sit at ladder levels 1-5 and score refusals or process-level safety with LLM judges or simulator checkers.
- Driving has a mature adversarial-scenario-generation line (AdvSim, STRIVE, KING, Adv3D, SAFE-SIM, ChatScene, LD-Scene, LLM-attacker, SEAL) at levels 3-5, scored by induced collision rate and by downstream training gains; physical-world perception attacks (RP2, Dirty Road, MSF-ADV, Phantom) reach levels 6-8 on production stacks.
- Standards: no standard prescribes an adversarial test protocol for robots. NIST AI 100-2e2025 supplies vocabulary only. ISO/PAS 8800 could not be fetched and is not catalogued.

Sim-to-real ladder coverage
- Level 0-1 (text/image QA): RoboJailBench, EARBench, MSSBench, BadRobot benchmark queries. Cheap, API-only, explicitly no humans or robots at risk.
- Level 3 (generated scenes, open-loop): Predictive Red Teaming, LD-Scene.
- Level 4-5 (closed-loop sim): the bulk of VLA attack work (LIBERO), ERT/DAERT, SafeAgentBench (AI2-THOR), IS-Bench (OmniGibson), driving scenario generation (CARLA, nuPlan replay).
- Level 6 (HIL): Dirty Road (real-vehicle trace injection), MSF-ADV (3D-printed object captured by real sensors).
- Level 7 (real robot, lab): RoboPAIR (Jackal, Go2), BadRobot (UR3e, myCobot), RedVLA and AttackVLA and DropVLA (Franka), UADA patches (UR10e), partially observable patches (ROKAE xMate), TrojanRobot (UR3e), MuTRAP (UR5e), Geometric Red-Teaming (xArm 6, Franka), RoboGuard (Jackal).
- Level 8 (field with surrogates): RP2 drive-by test, Phantom of the ADAS on a Tesla Model X.
- Level 9: nothing found. No paper reports deployment monitoring data for adversarial events.

Where cost evidence is strong or absent
- Almost universally absent in dollars or hours. Usable fragments: RoboGCG 3-10 min per successful attack on an H100; DP-Attacker 1.3-1.8 s per online perturbation; Geometric Red-Teaming 22 s per mesh deformation on an RTX 4090, 7-9 search iterations, plus PLA printing; IS-Bench 2.5-3 h per model evaluation on one A100; RoboGuard 12-21 percent of attacker tokens and 1 LLM query vs 15; Predictive Red Teaming 500+ hardware trials for validation vs zero for prediction; FATE-VLA states real-robot failure seeking is impractical due to cost, reset effort and safety. Trial counts (35 per robot for RoboPAIR, 100-200 real trials for patch and AttackVLA work, 10 trials per task for RedVLA) are the best proxy for hardware cost.
- Human labelling cost is reported only as counts: EARBench 200 items x 5 annotators; SafeAgentBench full manual review of 750 tasks; IS-Bench manual execution of every reference plan; RoboPAIR two expert annotators on every jailbreak.

Where validity evidence is strong or absent
- Strongest: Predictive Red Teaming (predicted vs measured success across 12 conditions, mean abs error 0.10-0.19, Spearman 0.7-0.8); Geometric Red-Teaming (four paired sim/real success rates, direction transfers, magnitude does not); patch attacks report a consistent sim-to-real ASR gap (UADA near 100 percent sim vs 43 percent real; UPA-RFAS 5.75 vs 40.25 percent success; AttackVLA attacks weaker on hardware), which is itself evidence that level-4 ASRs over-predict physical risk. RP2 gives the same lesson for perception (100 percent lab vs 84.8 percent drive-by).
- Judge validity: SafeAgentBench GPT-4 judge agrees with humans 90-92 percent; EARBench kappa 0.48 without and 0.85 with safety tips; BadRobot and most jailbreak papers use LLM judges without agreement statistics.
- Absent: no study links any adversarial ASR to incident rates, insurance data or operational safety outcomes (level 9). No false-alarm analysis exists for red-teaming methods (all report hits, none report the fraction of found failures that are spurious or unrealistic, except RedVLA and STRIVE which add plausibility filters without quantifying precision).

Canonical entries for a 4-page paper (5-8)
1. robey2024jailbreaking (RoboPAIR): the reference jailbreak with real robots, 100 percent ASR, human-checked judge.
2. karnik2024embodied (ERT): the reference automated red-teaming evaluation of language-conditioned policies.
3. majumdar2025predictive (Predictive Red Teaming): the only entry that quantifies how well a cheap evaluation predicts hardware outcomes; direct VOI evidence; Sindhwani co-author.
4. goel2025geometric (Geometric Red-Teaming): paired sim/real numbers and per-shape compute cost.
5. li2025attackvla (AttackVLA): cross-attack, cross-model, sim and real comparison of 9 attacks.
6. zhang2026redvla (RedVLA): physical-safety red teaming with predicate scoring and hardware reproduction.
7. yin2024safeagentbench (SafeAgentBench): planner-safety benchmark with judge-human agreement figures.
8. sinha2026rethinking (Rethinking Safety for Generalist Robots): framing; Bajcsy co-author. Optionally sato2021dirty or eykholt2018robust for the physical-world ADAS lineage.

Caveats
- Web search budget was exhausted after three rounds; the third round still yielded new items, so the catalogue is not provably saturated. Coverage of 2026 preprints (DURA, FlowHijack, Beyond the Patch, VILTA) is abstract-only and marked low confidence.
- Several affiliations are inferred from author lists and flagged 'unverified'.
